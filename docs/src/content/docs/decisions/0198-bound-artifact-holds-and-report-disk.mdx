---
title: "0198: Bound artifact holds by time and report held bytes and free disk"
description: "ADR: holds lapse after a renewable window, held bytes get an advisory budget, and artifact commands report free disk and refuse new large work below a floor."
---

**Status:** accepted (2026-10-05) · **Scope:** working artifacts (`artifacts hold`, `list`, `clean`, `create`) · **Related:** [0150](/decisions/0150-bound-local-coordination-storage/), [0193](/decisions/0193-idle-grace-for-artifact-size-rules/)

## Context

A hold makes a workspace `managed-held`: cleanup never deletes it, and it is
excluded from the repository budget and the per-workspace ceiling. Holds had no
expiry and no budget of their own, so protection outlived the work it was meant
to cover. On one busy host, held artifacts grew to 100 GB on a 500 GB disk.
Nothing reported the growth until the disk was nearly full, because every size
rule deliberately skipped held work.

## Alternatives considered

- Count held bytes in the main repository budget. Cleanup can never remove held
  work, so the extra pressure would evict unheld workspaces without reducing
  the held total.
- Delete held work once held bytes exceed a budget. A hold records its owner's
  intent that the files must survive; deleting material under review or awaiting
  a handoff loses work that cannot be rebuilt.
- Give existing holds an expiry retroactively. Holds written before this change
  would lapse at once and their workspaces could be deleted at the next
  cleanup, with no notice to their owners.

## Decision

Holds lapse. A new hold records `expires_at`, by default `artifacts.hold_days`
(14) days after it is set; `hold --days` or `--minutes` chooses another window.
Repeating `hold` with the same id, owner, and reason renews it from now. A
different owner or reason still fails. Embedding hosts whose hold mirrors an
external lease, such as an open checkout with unsynchronized work, pass
`persistent: true` through the API, or `--hold-persistent` on `create` and
`--persistent` on `hold`, because embedding hosts that run as separate processes
reach the store through the CLI. That hold never lapses and still counts toward
the held budget, so the budget warning keeps an abandoned lease visible.

Only holds in force make a workspace `managed-held`. When every hold has lapsed,
the workspace returns to ordinary retention. Its effective expiry and its
size-rule idle clock both count from no earlier than the latest lapse, and an
expired row names the lapsed holds. Lapsed holds stay in the manifest so their
owner can remove or renew them. Holds recorded before this change have no
`expires_at` and stay in force, with a warning that re-holding starts an expiry.
A hold that lapses within 48 hours carries a warning with the exact renewal
command.

Held bytes get an advisory budget, `artifacts.max_held_bytes` (20 GiB). Above
it, `list` and `clean` report `held_over_budget`, the five largest held
workspaces with their holds, and a plain warning. Nothing is deleted.

Artifact commands report free disk on the store's filesystem. Below
`artifacts.min_free_bytes` (20 GiB), `list`, `clean`, `create`, and `hold` warn,
and `create --big` refuses unless `--allow-low-disk` is passed. Every full
inventory records held bytes and free disk in `.harnery/artifact-usage.json`,
so `create` and `hold` can warn from that cache and a live free-space reading
without walking the store.

## Consequences

Held growth is now bounded by time unless an owner keeps renewing, and
visible before the disk fills. Long reviews need a renewal every two weeks or a
longer `--days`. The held budget and the disk floor never delete anything, so a
store can still fill if owners ignore the warnings; the warnings name the
workspaces and commands that free space. Holds written before this change stay
unbounded until their owners renew or remove them. The cached held total can be
stale by up to one cleanup interval; the free-disk reading is always live.
