**Date:** 2026-10-26
**Status:** Accepted

## Context

Each tile in a page review is judged by a separate model session. A host
usually routes those sessions to a subscription-backed headless harness, the
same subscription its interactive agents work in. The tile count is therefore
the cost of a run, and it scaled with page length times the number of
rendering contexts.

Two contracts let that grow without limit. The `qa-run` job accepted a tile
budget of up to 400. Signoff required every band of every context, so on a long
page the only way to reach a pass was to raise the budget to the maximum and
rerun. A page 148,528 px tall on mobile needed 384 tiles in four contexts, and
repeated signoff attempts launched hundreds of sessions in an hour, exhausting
the subscription that the operator's own sessions depended on. Separately,
`browse --check-critique-max-tiles` and `review-pack create --max-tiles` had no
upper bound, `tilesFromFullPage` could add 50 gate-hit bands past any cap, and
`review-pack judge` sent every tile in a pack to the provider.

## Alternatives considered

- **Lower the default budget only.** Rejected: the default was already 96. The
  growth came from jobs that raised the budget because signoff demanded it.
- **Keep full-coverage signoff and only lower the maximum.** Rejected: a page
  above the maximum could then never sign off, and agents would loop on
  reruns that cannot succeed.
- **Let any capped signoff pass.** Rejected: a budget that silently skipped a
  band with a gate hit, or a required scope, would pass the regions most
  likely to be broken.
- **Leave spend limits to the host.** Partly adopted: hosts can and should cap
  sessions across runs, but every host would need to rediscover the per-run
  bound, and the signoff contract is Harnery's.

## Decision

`PAGE_REVIEW_MAX_TILE_BUDGET` is 160 and is a hard per-invocation ceiling.
`policy.critique_tile_budget`, `policy.critique_max_tiles`, and `qa-run
--tile-budget` reject larger values. `browse --check-critique-max-tiles` is
clamped to it, and gate-hit bands added past the cap never take the total
above it. `review-pack create --max-tiles` accepts at most 160 and shares the
ceiling across contexts. `runCritique` and `judgePageReviewPack` refuse a tile
set above the ceiling without calling the provider; the judge reports every
context `incomplete` with the reason.

Signoff keeps its full-coverage requirement when the plans for all contexts
fit under the ceiling. When they need more, complete coverage is unavailable
by policy, and signoff accepts a capped critique as a sampled review if the
allocation kept every context's top and bottom band, every band carrying a
gate hit, and every required scope. The `qa-run` critique row records
`coverage_mode` (`full`, `capped`, or `sampled`). The pack's critique record
carries `coverage_mode: "sampled"` so `resolvePackVerdict` treats the context
as covered, and re-judging the pack keeps the marker. A context without an
allocation record cannot prove its required regions and stays a blocker. A
sampled critique is never persisted as a baseline's finding record, and durable
reviewer decisions still require complete coverage.

## Result and limits

Focused tests cover the validator bounds, the tiler clamp, the `runCritique`
and judge refusals, a judge at exactly the ceiling, sampled signoff on a
170-band page, refusal when a gate hit was left out, refusal without an
allocation record, the pack verdict with and without the marker, and the
`review-pack create` rejection.

The ceiling bounds one invocation, not a sequence of them. Several runs, or
several agents, can still each spend up to 160 calls. Bounding spend across
runs belongs to the host's provider, which knows which subscription each call
uses. On a long page, the uncovered intervals are reviewed only by the
deterministic gates, so a visual defect that no gate detects can pass a
sampled signoff.
