**Date:** 2026-10-18
**Status:** Superseded by [ADR 0221](./0221-restore-spawn-retries.mdx)

## Context

A child can change files or produce part of an answer before its provider
returns a transport error. A 429 or 503 identifies a failure cause, but does not
establish that the attempt did no work. Discarding failed output also loses
information needed to decide whether retrying is safe.

[ADR 0046](./0046-uninformative-attempts-go-uncharged.mdx) keeps positively
uninformative attempts outside the work budget. That policy needs evidence
across adapter normalization, retry decisions, workflow proof and durable work
charging, rather than trust in a transport class alone.

## Alternatives considered

- Treat every provider error as free and retryable. This can repeat effects
  and discard completed work.
- Charge every failure. This loses the existing known-unstarted process path.
- Infer no effects or refusal from status codes, empty output or prose. Those
  observations do not carry that authority.
- Introduce a separate event ledger. The existing spawn and workflow proof
  contracts can carry the needed observations without a new ledger protocol.

## Decision

`SpawnFailureEvidence` records effects, refusal and partial output as separate
observations. Unknown is explicit. Positive effects/refusal claims require a
declared source and reference; persisted proof readers validate their structure.
That validation does not establish the truth of an adapter's claim. Any custom
adapter producer must document the observation behind its positive evidence.

The registered subprocess adapters currently prove no effects only for a
positively unstarted `ENOENT` result with no contradictory output or timeout.
Their supported error envelopes do not prove native refusal or absence of
effects. Those states remain unknown. Final-message, result-envelope and text
stream output survive failure, with source, byte count and digest in proof.
Text that sounds like a refusal is not promoted to native refusal authority.

Automatic redispatch and uncharged transport failures require positive
no-effect evidence, observed absent output, and no refusal. The known missing
binary remains uncharged and stopped. A declared refusal, observed effects,
partial output or uncertainty prevents automatic redispatch and free work.

Every dispatched participant matters. A successful or cached sibling charges
the attempt, as does an earlier informative spawn whose result failed schema
validation. Empty participant sets cannot establish no effects. Durable work
charging re-derives eligibility from the agents' proof instead of trusting
`run.class`. Missing evidence has no legacy exemption. The script's existing
explicit decision authority remains separate.

This changes the workflow module's existing types and proof data. It adds no
coordination-ledger event, schema migration, provider call or compatibility
reader.

## Verification and limits

Regression coverage retains real fixture-file effects, partial output,
unknown evidence, known-unstarted processes, refusal dominance, sibling work,
persisted evidence validation and repeated-attempt charging. Vendor-format
fixtures exercise the registered parsers; they are not attestations of live
provider events. A provider-specific positive effect/refusal producer still
needs its own documented native observation.

## Scope and promotion

This contract belongs to failed workflow spawns and their durable work
consumers. It does not establish provider billing, native compaction outcomes
or authority for unrelated coordination operations.
